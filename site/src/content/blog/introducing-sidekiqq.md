Most of what slips through the cracks in a busy week isn't lost. It's sitting in an email, written down in plain words, waiting for someone to notice that it was a promise.

"I'll get you pricing by Wednesday." "Let's talk again next week." "We'll send the deposit Monday." Each one is a small commitment, made in passing, and each one becomes a problem if nobody remembers it.

## A chief of staff, not another inbox

The people who handle this well usually have help: someone who reads everything, remembers who said what, and taps them on the shoulder before a date slips. That's the job SideKiqq is built to do.

It reads your email and calendar. It works out which messages matter and which client they belong to. It pulls out every commitment, yours and other people's, with a due date, and it labels the date as a guess when it had to guess. When something is about to slip, it tells you, and it has a reply drafted.

## It never sends without you

The draft is where SideKiqq stops. It doesn't send anything. Every message waits until you've read it and said yes, on your phone, on your watch, or out loud with a second confirmation. An assistant that writes in your name has to earn trust slowly, and the simplest way to do that is to never act alone.

## On every device

You're not always at a desk, so SideKiqq isn't either. The phone app holds the detail. A Galaxy Watch shows the five things that matter today. With Galaxy Glasses you can ask a question and hear a short answer. A small medallion with a single button lets you capture a thought without taking anything out of your pocket. A Reachy Mini desk robot is next on the list.

They're all windows onto one assistant, with one memory of your clients and commitments.

## Where things stand

SideKiqq is in private development. The assistant, the phone and watch apps, and the medallion firmware are written, and testing on real hardware is next. If you'd like to try it when it's ready, [join the waitlist](/#waitlist).
